# Kerala circa 2000: implementation direction

User steering, 14 September 2026: continue the four-region implementation and make the world recognizably Kerala in the 2000s, using the second supplied map as the latest composition reference.

This supersedes the temporary one-zone scope in the first foundation loop. Preserve the world topology and shared interface/art guardrails. Render a stylized 3D explorer with an early-console adventure sensibility, set in small-town Kerala around 2000–2005. This is an interpretation of the brief, not a historical reconstruction.

## Recognizable environmental cues

- Coconut palms with arching fronds, banana leaves, paddy bunds, pepper vines, dense highland trees.
- Laterite compound walls, off-white plaster, red oxide verandas, clay-tile sloping/hipped roofs, deep overhangs.
- Kerala temple courtyard with low, layered tile-roof shrine, brass lamp, and temple tank. Avoid a generic tall north-Indian temple tower.
- Narrow dark tar roads with sandy shoulders and drainage edges; no wide multi-lane village boulevards.
- Tea shop, provision store, bakery, stationery shop; painted Malayalam/English boards, timber benches, shutters, corrugated awnings, modest utility poles and wires.
- A wide blue-green Kurumali river with visible banks, wooden boats and one wooden crossing. The field map shares the river and bridge coordinates.
- Compact Kodaly harbor with low colonial-era facades, a few plain concrete buildings, tiled roofs and lighthouse.
- No smartphone adverts, delivery-app signage, glass towers, neon sci-fi UI, or luxury resort styling.

## Implementation and review

Build a compressed, connected prototype (about 166×576 m) to visit the landmarks without long empty walks. The original larger envelope remains a future expansion target. Current art remains original procedural prototype geometry; finished GLB rigs/textures and the full asset art gate are not complete.

Astra owns terrain, water/bank topology, traversal, physics, architecture and integration. Luna owns bounded captions, map labels, simple content data, and contract tests. The user has authorized continued implementation.
